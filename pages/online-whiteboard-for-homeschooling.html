Homeschool Uses Across Every Subject

How homeschooling families use the whiteboard across the curriculum.

➕

Maths — Visual Explanations

Draw fractions, geometry diagrams, and worked calculations by hand — just like a physical whiteboard. The pen replicates natural handwriting and you can erase and redo as needed. Export worked examples as PDF revision sheets.

📜

History — Timelines

Build illustrated history timelines — draw a horizontal axis with the line tool, add event sticky notes along it, and upload relevant images. Save each timeline as a PDF and build a visual history archive your child can review.

🌿

Nature Study — Observation Boards

Upload photos from nature walks and annotate them — label parts of a flower, identify bird features, sketch the life cycle alongside the photo. These annotated nature boards become a running nature journal in PDF form.

🗺️

Geography — Annotated Maps

Upload a blank map and label countries, capitals, rivers, and mountain ranges using the text and pen tools. Make it interactive — add sticky notes with facts about each region as you study.

📚

Literature — Story & Theme Maps

Build character maps and theme boards for books you're reading together. Sticky notes for characters, arrows showing relationships, colour-coded themes. A visual approach to literature that suits visual learners especially well.

🎨

Creative Drawing & Art

The calligraphy tool, colour picker, and freehand pen let children draw freely on a large canvas — make patterns, illustrate stories, or practice lettering. Export their artwork as PNG to print or save.

Building a Lesson Archive Over Time

How to use the whiteboard as a record of your homeschool year.

One of the most useful homeschooling habits with the whiteboard is building a PDF archive. At the end of each lesson, export the canvas as a PDF named by date and topic — for example 2025-03-history-romans.pdf. Over a term or year, this becomes a complete visual record of everything covered, useful for portfolio reviews and for your child to look back on.

Multiple boards per lesson: Use a separate board for each lesson section — introduction, worked examples, review. Switch between boards mid-lesson and keep everything organised within a single session.
Reopen and continue: Save the project file at the end of a lesson and reopen it next time to pick up exactly where you left off — great for multi-day projects like timelines that build over several sessions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this completely free with no hidden subscription?

Yes — no subscription, no trial that expires, no premium tier. Every feature including PDF export, sticky notes, image upload, and multiple boards is free forever with no account required.

Is it safe for children to use?

Yes — no account means no personal data collected. No social features, no internet connectivity once loaded, no advertising. It's a private drawing canvas that exists only in the browser session. No child data of any kind is processed.

Can I use it without an internet connection after it loads?

Once the page has fully loaded, basic drawing features continue to work offline. The initial load requires an internet connection, but you can draw, annotate, and save locally without staying connected.

How do I keep my lesson boards organised over many months?

Save each lesson's project file with a descriptive name and date before closing. Create a folder on your device per subject. Export PDFs into the same folder for a searchable visual archive of your homeschool year.
