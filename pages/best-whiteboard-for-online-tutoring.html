What Tutors Actually Need from a Whiteboard

The criteria that matter in a real tutoring session — not a demo.

✏️

Natural Drawing Quality

Maths and science tutoring involves writing equations and drawing diagrams live. The pen tool must respond smoothly — lag, jitter, or poor stroke quality makes it harder to write legibly than on paper. This is non-negotiable for STEM subjects.

📺

Clean Screen Share in Zoom/Meet

The whiteboard needs to render sharply when shared via Zoom or Google Meet. Tools with heavy UI chrome, animated loading elements, or ads in the shared view distract from the teaching content.

👨‍🎓

Zero Friction for Students

Students shouldn't need an account, download, or setup to see the whiteboard — that's the tutor's job to manage. Ideally the student just watches your screen share, with no tool interaction required on their end.

📄

Session Notes Export

After a session, the tutor should be able to export the canvas as a PDF and send it to the student. This turns the whiteboard session into a revision resource. Export should be free and watermark-free.

🖼️

Image Upload for Homework Annotation

Students send photos of their homework, worksheets, or textbook problems. The tutor uploads the image to the canvas and annotates it directly — correcting errors, adding worked examples alongside the original.

💾

Session Continuity Between Lessons

Saving and reloading a student's board between sessions means you can pick up exactly where you left off — reviewing last week's corrections before moving on. No wasted time reconstructing previous work.

Top Whiteboards for Online Tutoring — Compared

Rated on the six criteria above.

🥇

OnlineWhiteboard.org — Best Free Pick

Cost: Free forever | Account: Not required

The top free option for tutors who need a capable whiteboard without a subscription. Pen drawing is smooth and responsive, screen sharing works cleanly in Zoom, Meet, and Teams, image upload lets you annotate homework directly, and PDF export is free with no watermark. Sessions save as project files and reload exactly — useful for tracking a student's progress across weekly lessons.

Best for: Independent tutors, home tutors, and anyone who wants a professional-grade tool at zero cost. Works in any browser on any device — no app needed.

Limitation: Single-user canvas — student can't draw on it simultaneously (though they can open their own and share screen for interactive practice).

🥈

Explain Everything — Best for Recorded Lessons

Cost: Varies by plan | Account: Typically required

Explain Everything is a popular app for tutors who record video explanations alongside the whiteboard. The screencasting feature records your voice and drawing simultaneously, producing a video lesson file students can rewatch. Drawing quality is excellent with Apple Pencil on iPad.

Best for: Tutors who create recorded explanations and video homework help, or who teach primarily via asynchronous video.

Limitation: Subscription cost, app required (not browser-based), and the screencasting focus adds complexity if you just need a live whiteboard.

🥉

Miro — Best for Complex Visual Planning

Cost: Varies by plan | Account: Typically required

Miro is known for template support, sticky note organisation, and an infinite canvas. For tutors who run structured sessions with frameworks (retrospectives, planning boards, visual study guides) it's well-suited. Board limits depend on your Miro plan, so check Miro's website for current details.

Best for: Tutors running structured visual frameworks, test prep planning, and multi-session study planning with older students.

Limitation: Collaboration typically involves accounts, and export options vary by plan. Freehand drawing quality is not as strong as purpose-built whiteboard tools.

4️⃣

Zoom Whiteboard — Best for Zoom-Only Sessions

Cost: Depends on your Zoom plan | Account: Zoom account typically required

If you and your students already use Zoom, the built-in whiteboard is convenient — no extra tool to open. Both tutor and student can draw simultaneously in the same session. The drawing tools are more limited than dedicated whiteboard tools and export options are basic, but for simple diagram-based explanations it removes one tool from the workflow.

Best for: Tutors and students who both use Zoom and want a simple in-call drawing surface without switching apps.

Limitation: Available features depend on your Zoom plan. Drawing tools are more limited compared to dedicated whiteboards. Session notes export is basic.

5️⃣

iPad + Apple Pencil + Any Browser Whiteboard

Cost: iPad cost only | Account: Depends on whiteboard chosen

For tutors who want the most natural handwriting experience, the setup is: iPad + Apple Pencil + OnlineWhiteboard.org in Safari. The Pencil provides pressure-sensitive, lag-free inking that feels close to writing on paper. Share the iPad screen to your Zoom call via AirPlay or cable. This combination gives professional drawing quality at zero software cost.

Best for: STEM tutors (maths, physics, chemistry) who write equations and diagrams heavily and want pen-quality that a mouse can't match.

Limitation: Requires iPad hardware investment. Slightly more complex screen sharing setup vs. sharing a laptop window.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do students need to create an account to use the whiteboard?

With OnlineWhiteboard.org — no. Students only need to watch your screen share. No account, no URL to visit, no software install required on the student's side. This eliminates the most common source of session startup delays.

Which whiteboard is best for maths tutoring specifically?

For handwritten equations and geometry, the iPad + Apple Pencil setup with OnlineWhiteboard.org in Safari gives the most natural writing experience. On a laptop, OnlineWhiteboard.org with a drawing tablet (Wacom or similar) connected is the next best option for maths.

Can I send session notes to students after each lesson?

Yes — with OnlineWhiteboard.org, click PDF to export the full session canvas. This becomes your student's lesson notes document. Send it via email, WhatsApp, or upload to whatever platform you use to communicate with students.

How do I keep separate boards per student?

Save each student's whiteboard session as a separate project file named by student and date. Reopen the relevant file at the start of each session. Over time this builds a chronological record of each student's sessions.
